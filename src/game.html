For grown-ups

How it teaches

  • Act 1 teaches the home row, the space bar (with the first words, where it's needed), and E and I, so early words aren't stuck with one vowel. Act 2 adds R U T O N W Y. By the end of Act 2 the captain can type 75 of the 100 most common English words. Act 3 finishes the alphabet (C M P B V X Q Z), then capitals and the punctuation in real sentences: . , ' ?. Each new key starts with reach drills, then real words, then silly phrases, because typists get fastest on real words.
  • The right pinky rests on the ; key but is never asked to type it. Kids almost never need a semicolon, so those practice minutes go to letters.
  • Shift is taught the standard way: hold it with the pinky on the opposite hand from the letter. If Caps Lock gets switched on by accident, the game still works and shows a reminder to turn it off.
  • Speed medals are optional extras for replays: 5, 8 and 12 words a minute in Acts 1, 2 and 3. They never block progress. Auto speed sets the spud pace from the captain's recent speed.
  • Each key and the finger that presses it share a color. As a key gets reliable, its hints fade so eyes stay on the screen. A mistake brings the hint back for a few tries.
  • Keys the captain misses most come back more often. After the finale, Candy Runs mix words from every mission and lean on the captain's weakest keys, which makes them good daily practice.
  • Stars reward aim: 3 at 95% or better, 2 at 90%, and 2 stars unlock the next mission. That matches school keyboarding benchmarks, which ask for 90 to 100% accuracy. Missed spuds never end a mission; they return later.
  • Short and frequent works best: one or two missions a day, 10 to 15 minutes.
